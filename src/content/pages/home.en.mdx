## What is Narinig Mo Ba?

Narinig Mo Ba is described by its creators as a **story-driven psychological horror game disguised as a cozy sari-sari store simulator**. That description is more important than any single genre label. The store simulation is not a separate minigame attached to the narrative; it is how the narrative reaches you.

Customers come to the counter because they need something. While you find products and complete transactions, you also hear what people are saying about the neighborhood. Small details accumulate. A normal shift becomes less normal, and the player begins to understand why the official description talks about hidden secrets, a tragic whispered truth, and a desperate act of survival that causes a chain reaction.

The itch.io listing classifies Narinig Mo Ba as a Visual Novel and tags it with Horror, Filipino, Short, Working Simulator, storygame, 2D, Cute, and No AI. Those tags explain the unusual tone: it can look warm and playful while still building psychological tension.

## How Narinig Mo Ba plays

The basic loop is intentionally easy to understand. A customer asks for one or more products. You look around the small shop, pick up the requested goods, check prices when needed, bring the order to the counter, handle payment and change, and then continue the conversation.

The released game is mouse-first. Left click is used for taking or placing items, holding left click can return an item, and the right-click interaction is used to inspect prices. Looking around the store depends on pointer movement, which is one reason proper touchscreen support takes more work than simply shrinking the game to a phone screen.

The most useful Narinig Mo Ba tip is to move slowly. Several players on the official itch.io discussion mention sensitive camera movement, and one player said careful mouse movement made the game comfortable enough to finish. There is no benefit to spinning quickly between shelves.

More importantly, do not treat the dialogue as filler. Narinig Mo Ba uses conversation as part of the gameplay. The shop task gives each visitor a reason to appear; what they say gives the player the information needed to understand the larger story.

## Why a sari-sari store works so well for horror

A sari-sari store is both a shop and a social crossing point. Neighbors stop by for small everyday purchases, and conversation happens naturally at the counter. That makes it an ideal setting for a game whose title means roughly “Did you hear?” or “Have you heard?”

In Narinig Mo Ba, the player is placed in the position of a listener. You do not begin as a detective who already knows something terrible happened. You begin by working. The neighborhood arrives in fragments, carried by customers and gossip.

That setup creates a useful kind of uncertainty. Hearing something is not the same as witnessing it. A rumor can contain truth, exaggeration, misunderstanding, or fear. Psychological horror can use that gap without needing to explain every strange moment immediately.

The store also creates a physical boundary between the player and the street, but it is a porous boundary. People can approach. Information can enter. The official description's image of the store being “left open both ways” gives that ordinary architecture a more threatening meaning.

## The four-day Game Jam origin

Game Jam Los Baños 2026 is a major part of the Narinig Mo Ba development story. The official jam submissions page lists 30 entries, including Narinig Mo Ba. In the project's itch.io comments, karinasaurus confirmed that the team made the game in four days as a jam entry and did not expect it to become widely popular.

Other creator replies describe the team as college students and mostly first-time game developers. That context helps explain why the game is compact and why players have reported occasional rough edges around camera movement, loading, or interactions.

It also makes the response to Narinig Mo Ba notable. In a September 2026 devlog, VC Studios said the game had recently gone viral across social media in the Philippines. The team thanked people who featured it, played it, and donated, and said the reception made them more optimistic about continuing to build games.

## What kind of horror is Narinig Mo Ba?

Narinig Mo Ba does not need to begin with constant danger because its strongest tool is contrast. The creators deliberately pair Cute and Horror tags. The shop, artwork, products, and casual conversations establish a relaxed baseline, while later changes in atmosphere and story make that baseline feel unstable.

Players in the official comments frequently praise the audio and the transition from cozy daily life into tension. That makes headphones a good choice, but the larger point is pacing: let scenes finish, listen before clicking again, and give quiet moments room to work.

If you already know the ending, a second playthrough can be useful because you can pay more attention to early dialogue rather than learning the store.
